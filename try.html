SEAL Sample Lab

Guided recipes for the current app.

The Sample Lab contains project-authored, deterministic synthetic records. These recipes cover translation, maps, cloning, alignment, Activity, recovery, and report handoff.

Start with Your first workspace to open the Sample Lab. Each recipe begins in that project. Click inside a sequence block to make it active; use Show inspector in the workspace header if its panel is hidden. The block’s Actions menu is beneath its sequence.

01 ~4 minutes

Live selection translation.

Use Synthetic Reporter DNA to see translation follow a nucleotide selection in real time.

  1. In the sidebar, expand SEAL Sample Lab and open Synthetic Reporter.
  2. Click inside Synthetic Reporter DNA.
  3. Drag across a base range, open Overview in the Inspector, and scroll to the Selection card.
  4. Select a different range of DNA and compare the updated translation.
  5. Check the amino-acid count, remainder, and internal-stop warnings.

What to notice: the readout reports frame, strand, selected range, genetic code, translated length, trailing bases, and stops without creating a new block.

✓ You should now see a selection-linked protein preview in the Inspector.

02 ~5 minutes

Six frames and reverse translation.

Compare every DNA reading frame, then turn a synthetic protein into a clearly labelled draft nucleotide sequence.

  1. With Synthetic Reporter DNA active, open the Inspector Translate tab.
  2. Compare +1, +2, +3, -1, -2, and -3 for the selection or whole block.
  3. Use Show, Copy, or New block on one frame.
  4. Click Synthetic Reporter Protein in the same entry and open Codons in the Inspector.
  5. In Back-translation preview, choose DNA or RNA, a host table, and a Codon strategy. Add enzyme names or motifs under Avoid sites, review the preview, then choose Draft candidate.

What to notice: Max-frequency is the default strategy; Balanced codon usage and Synthesis-guarded are also available. Unencodable symbols become NNN. Stop symbols receive codons, and later residues remain in the draft.

✓ You should now see a draft with length, GC, encoded-symbol counts, and notices for stops or unresolved sites.

03 ~5 minutes

Linked maps and synthetic backbone review.

Compare circular DNA, linear DNA, and protein maps, then inspect a deterministic synthetic cloning fixture.

  1. In the sidebar, open Cloning & Assembly and click SEAL Demo Plasmid. Its circular map starts above the DNA.
  2. Open the arrow beside Map in the block header and try Map only, Map below sequence, and Open floating map.
  3. In the sidebar, open Synthetic Genomic Region to find SEAL Synthetic Genomic Region (30 kb). Then open Synthetic Protein Models to find Synthetic Modular Peptide Model. Both use linear maps.
  4. Click Browse vectors beside the composer. Select SEAL Synthetic Cloning Backbone, then click Add to workspace.
  5. On the added DNA block, open Actions → Restriction digest to inspect cuts. Close the dialog, then choose Actions → Clone builder to inspect vector and insert compatibility.

What to notice: map placement changes how you view the record. Choose Sequence only in the Map menu to return to the sequence.

✓ You should now have compared map-only, linked, and floating layouts and reviewed an in-silico digest preview.

04 ~8 minutes

Golden Gate and GoldenBraid assembly.

Follow a complete synthetic example across parts, product, Activity, and a linked notebook.

  1. In the sidebar, open Cloning & Assembly.
  2. Scroll through the entry to read its SEAL assembly notebook note block.
  3. Inspect the three synthetic Level 0 parts and the Level 1 destination backbone.
  4. Click SEAL Golden Gate Sample Assembly, open the Inspector’s Activity tab, and review the assembly event.
  5. Open a DNA block’s Actions menu and choose Golden Gate or GoldenBraid assembly. Use the cloning guide to configure a new assembly and compare it with the sample product.

What to notice: parts, backbones, products, and notebook records stay linked as one synthetic example.

✓ You should now see the product linked to its synthetic inputs and local records.

05 ~8 minutes

30 kb synthetic operon region.

Use when you want to test larger synthetic-region handling and parent-child coordinates.

  1. In the sidebar, open Synthetic Genomic Region.
  2. Select SEAL Synthetic Genomic Region (30 kb).
  3. Compare the parent features with extracted CDS and protein blocks.
  4. Open Features in the Inspector and inspect annotations such as sensorA, reporterB, and Synthetic promoter-like region.
  5. Select the region and its derived blocks using their header checkboxes, then click Add to report in the selection toolbar.

What to notice: coordinates, linked CDS features, child blocks, and protein translations stay tied to the parent interval.

✓ You should now see the synthetic 30 kb region and its lineage-linked child blocks.

06 ~3 minutes

Sequence formatting and print.

Apply presentation metadata without changing the biological sequence.

  1. Select a range in any synthetic DNA, RNA, or protein block.
  2. Open the Display Inspector tab.
  3. Scroll to the Highlights card and click Add. A new region highlight appears on your selected range.
  4. Press ⌘K, type Build report, and select the command to open Report handoff.
  5. Under Attachments, select the styled report and biological formats, then click Download ZIP. Open the styled HTML to review the highlight and compare it with the sequence exports.

What to notice: raw sequence, coordinates, translation, extraction, FASTA, GenBank, and CSV remain biologically clean.

✓ You should now see presentation styling in the report but not in biological exports.

07 ~7 minutes

Sample MSA and synthetic Sanger traces.

Inspect a ready reference, two controlled variants, and paired synthetic chromatograms without importing a file.

  1. In the sidebar, open Sanger Reads & Alignment in the SEAL Sample Lab.
  2. Use the header checkboxes to select Synthetic Amplicon Reference, Synthetic Full-length SNP Variant, and Synthetic Full-length +3 bp Variant. Click Align in the selection toolbar, check the three staged names, then click Align 3.
  3. Close the alignment, clear the previous header checkboxes, and select only Synthetic Amplicon Reference and Synthetic Sanger Forward Read. Click Align, then Align 2. Beneath the result, click Verify against…; the button names your reference.
  4. Close the alignment and click the forward read block. Open Trace in the Inspector to inspect its four channels and quality values.
  5. Clear the header checkboxes, then select only Synthetic Sanger Forward Read and Synthetic Sanger Reverse Read. Click Align, then Align 2. Beneath the result, click Build consensus contig to reconstruct their synthetic overlap.

What to notice: the controlled forward read carries one low-quality probable miscall and one high-quality mismatch. The app reserves 100% identity for exact agreement and does not promote either result into an automatically confirmed variant.

✓ You should now have reviewed an MSA, a four-channel synthetic trace, and a quality-weighted synthetic consensus.

08 ~10 minutes

External-agent dry run.

Connect a source checkout to Codex, Claude Code, Gemini CLI, or another MCP client without granting an immediate write.

  1. Use the Node.js version in .nvmrc and run npm ci in a SEAL source checkout.
  2. Configure the checkout-local node_modules/.bin/tsx, mcp/server.ts, and explicit SEAL_DB_PATH.
  3. In the app, press ⌘K, type Run agent doctor, and select the command.
  4. Ask the agent to call block_list for shared sequence blocks, then inspect projects, entries, and recent Activity without writing.
  5. Compare the returned block names and lengths with the app. For a later editing session, use the agent prompt examples to plan a change and review its result.

What to notice: the DMG does not install the MCP runtime or Rust CLI. External prompts and context follow the privacy terms of the agent provider you choose.

✓ You should now have a verified read-only connection to the same workspace shown in SEAL.

09 ~4 minutes

Report package handoff.

Build a shareable local review package and understand which exports preserve metadata.

  1. Select one or more sequence blocks using their header checkboxes.
  2. Click Add to report in the selection toolbar to open Report handoff.
  3. Under Attachments, select the FASTA, GenBank, CSV, or styled report files you need. Review the report sections beneath them.
  4. For a styled report, choose a Preset. Click Download ZIP to save the package.
  5. Inspect manifest.json and validation.md.

What to notice: GenBank is metadata-preserving. FASTA and CSV are intentionally lossy summaries. Styled HTML is for review and print.

✓ You should now have a local zip with the selected formats and validation results.
